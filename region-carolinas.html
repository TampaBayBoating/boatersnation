New to Boating

New to boating? The 8 steps below, in the right order. No experience assumed.

1Get legal first

North Carolina and South Carolina run different rulebooks. In NC, anyone born on or after Jan. 1, 1988 must pass a NASBLA-approved course before running a motor of 10 hp or more. In SC, the course is required if you were born after July 1, 2007 (same 10 hp line). Both states register and title boats their own way too — tap through to the NC and SC sections of State Rules & Agencies for each state's official links.

→ 2Gear up: the safety checklist

Life jackets for everyone, throwable cushion, fire extinguisher, flares, horn, kill-switch lanyard. See exactly what the Coast Guard requires.

→ 3Rent before you buy

Try different boats through rentals or a boat club before spending a dollar. Learn what you actually like on the water first.

→ 4Learn the markers

“Red, right, returning” — channel markers, no-wake signs, and who has the right of way. The rules that keep everyone safe.

→ 5Launch, dock & anchor

Ramp etiquette, docking without the drama, knots that hold, and anchoring that actually sticks.

→ 6Respect Carolina waters

The inlets are the big one: the U.S. Coast Guard warns that shoaling exists at Oregon Inlet and 11 other North Carolina inlets, that the shoals shift continually — sometimes rapidly — and that inlet markers may not be marking the best water, so cross only with local knowledge and the latest Corps survey. Around Cape Hatteras the weather can turn fast where cold northern water meets the warm Gulf Stream offshore. The Intracoastal Waterway shoals up in known hotspots between dredging cycles — run the marked channel, not last year's memory of it. And Charleston runs one of the biggest tides on this coast: NOAA puts the harbor's average high-tide range at about 5½ feet, with strong currents in the harbor and its tidal creeks. Local knowledge that saves props — and lives.

→ 7Before every trip

Check the marine forecast, tell someone your float plan, and monitor VHF channel 16. If the inlet looks angry, stay in the sound.

→ 8Act like you've been here before

Boating etiquette — the ramp rules, guest rules, and sandbar manners that keep the regulars friendly and get you invited back.

→
Beginner mistakes that cost money

Running aground on a falling tide · crossing an inlet on last year's channel · boating with no tow coverage · buying the wrong boat before ever renting one.
